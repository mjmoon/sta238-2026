Conjugate priors

We will discussion the normal-normal pair and beta-binomial pairs.

While conjugate priors provide computational convenience, you do not need to restrict Bayesian models to conjugate pairs.


We say that a prior distribution \(p(\theta)\) is a conjugate prior for a statistical model \(p(\textbf x|\theta)\) if the posterior distribution \(p(\theta|\textbf x)\) is from the same probability distribution family as the prior distribution.

Normal-normal model

The following pair

Data model

\[X_1,\ldots,X_n |\theta \overset{\text{i.i.d}}{\sim} N(\theta, \sigma^2)\]

Prior

\[\theta \sim N(\phi, \tau^2)\]

leads to

Posterior distribution

\[\theta|\mathbf{x} \sim N\left( \phi \frac{\sigma^2}{n\tau^2+\sigma^2} + \overline{x}_n\frac{n\tau^2}{n\tau^2+\sigma^2}, \frac{\tau^2\sigma^2}{n\tau^2 + \sigma^2}\right)\]

  • The posterior mean is the weighted average between the sample mean and the prior mean

  • This is generally true in Bayesian updates; the Beta-binomial model is also shifts the posterior mean towards the sample mean of the proportion

Credible interval

A credible interval doesn’t need to be in the middle; an off-middle interval may represent the most plausible range better when the distribution is skewed.


A posterior credible interval provides a range of plausible values of a parameter based on its posterior distribution. A \(p\)% credible interval is any interval such that the posterior probability of the parameter falling within the interval is \(p\)%.

For example, the 2.5th and 97.5th posterior percentile is a middle 95% credible interval.

Example: Commute time

Posterior

\[\theta|\mathbf{t} \sim {N}\left(42, 3.2\right)\]

To construct a 90% credible interval for \(\theta\), we need to find \(a\) and \(b\) such that

\[P(a < \theta < b | \mathbf{t}) = 0.9\]

A case study: UK gender pay gap among Higher Education Employees

Data retrieved from Tidytuesday; original source at https://gender-pay-gap.service.gov.uk/.

UK gender pay gap data

  • In the UK, employers with 250 or more employees are required to report gender pay gap data annually.

  • They measure gender pay gap as percentage difference in median hourly wages among men minus those among women as shown on the right.

  • We will look at the pay gap data reported by higher education employers (e.g., universities) in 2022 to estimate the mean gender pay gap in higher education.

Data model

\[D_1, D_2,\ldots,D_n\overset{i.i.d}\sim {N}(\mu, \sigma^2)\]

Common features of the two intervals

  • With a large sample, the credible interval would shrink in width as the confidence interval does; and they both shrink towards the sample mean.

  • They both provide a range of plausible values for an unknown parameter.

Difference between the two intervals

  • Their key difference is the way they are interpreted.
  • The credible interval is the range of the possible values of the parameter.

There is a 95% probability \(\theta\) is within in the interval.

  • The confidence interval is the range which possibly includes a fixed true parameter value.

If we repeatedly the interval in the same manner indefinitely, 95% of them will include the true parameter value.

Summary

  • Priors are selected to represent prior knowledge/belief; there may be multiple ways to construct; allow positive probabilities for all possible values
  • Both frequentist and Bayesian inferences can provide point and interval estimates.
  • While the results may provide similar numeric estimates, the interpretations would be different since their assumptions about the parameters are different.
